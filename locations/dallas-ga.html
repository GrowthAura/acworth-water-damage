Why do crawl spaces in Dallas, GA homes stay wet after Paulding County storms?

Clay soil, sloped lots and vented crawl spaces let storm runoff pool under homes, where Georgia humidity keeps it from drying on its own.

Much of Paulding County is rolling, wooded land with red clay that drains slowly. Many Dallas homes sit on crawl spaces or are built into slopes, so after heavy rain, water runs toward the foundation and collects underneath. Vented crawl spaces then pull in humid outdoor air, and the moisture never really leaves. That's why crawl space water and musty smells are among the most common calls in Dallas, GA.

Crawl space water in Dallas, GA: what we do

Crawl space water in Dallas, GA homes gets handled in four steps. First, standing water is pumped out. Second, soaked insulation and torn or muddy vapor barrier are removed. Third, the crawl space is dried with commercial dehumidifiers and air movers while wood moisture is tracked. Fourth, insulation and vapor barrier are replaced once the joists and subfloor are dry. Long-term fixes like drainage, sump pumps or encapsulation are handled by crawl space contractors afterward.

Flooded basement in Dallas, GA

A flooded basement in Dallas, GA is usually a hillside problem. The uphill walls hold back clay soil that stays saturated after rain, and water pushes through block walls or the wall-floor joint. Sump pump failures during storms make it worse, especially when the power goes out. Finished basements need fast extraction, pad removal and commercial dehumidification to save the framing and most of the drywall.

Freezing nights and burst pipes in Paulding County

Rural and wooded properties in Paulding County can see colder overnight temperatures than more developed areas. Pipes in crawl spaces, unheated garages and exterior walls are the most exposed. Insulating crawl space pipes, sealing foundation vents during hard freezes, dripping faucets on the coldest nights and keeping heat on in vacant homes all help prevent a burst pipe.
