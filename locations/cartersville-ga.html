Crawl space water in Bartow County homes

Crawl space water is one of the most common Cartersville calls. Clay soil drains slowly, so after heavy rain, water seeps through foundation walls or pools on the crawl space floor. Vapor barriers trap water on top, insulation sags and humidity rises into the home. The fix starts with pumping out water, removing wet insulation and drying joists and subfloor with dehumidifiers, then keeping the space dry with drainage and a sound vapor barrier.

Lake homes near Red Top Mountain and Allatoona

Cartersville's side of Lake Allatoona, around Red Top Mountain and the eastern parts of 30121, includes lake homes and second homes. These face walkout basement seepage during long periods of high water, vacant-home leaks and storm damage from tall trees. See Lake Allatoona water damage restoration

Older homes and downtown Cartersville buildings

Older homes and commercial buildings in and around downtown Cartersville often have plaster walls, original hardwood, aging plumbing and basements or crawl spaces. Water damage there calls for careful drying to protect materials that are hard to replace. Plaster can sometimes be dried in place, and original hardwood often responds well to floor drying mats if drying starts quickly.
