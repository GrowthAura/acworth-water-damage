Flooded basement in Woodstock: the Cherokee County clay problem

A flooded basement in Woodstock usually comes back to the same cause: clay soil that holds water against the foundation. Many Towne Lake and established neighborhood homes are built on slopes with basements set into the hill. After several days of rain, that soil stays saturated and pushes water through the wall-floor joint or block walls. A failed sump pump or a downspout dumping next to the house can turn seepage into standing water fast. Extraction, carpet pad removal and commercial dehumidification keep most finished basements from becoming a full rebuild.

Sewage backup in Woodstock: city sewer vs. septic

Depending on the address, homes in and around Woodstock are on city sewer, county sewer or private septic systems. Responsibility generally works the same way on public systems: the utility maintains the mains, and the homeowner owns the private line from the house. On septic, the homeowner owns the whole system. Either way, sewage inside the home is Category 3 water and needs containment, removal of porous materials, disinfection and drying.

Signs your backup may be septic-related:

  • Slow drains throughout the home
  • Gurgling toilets
  • Odor near the drain field
  • Backups after heavy rain saturates the ground
