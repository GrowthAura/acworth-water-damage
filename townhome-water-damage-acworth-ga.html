Who pays when water leaks from one Acworth townhome into the next?

It depends on where the leak started and your HOA documents: the unit owner, the HOA master policy or the neighbor's insurance may each be responsible.

Most Acworth townhome communities split responsibility between the HOA and individual owners. The HOA usually covers common elements like the roof, exterior walls and shared plumbing, while owners usually cover their own interior, fixtures and belongings. The exact split is written in your HOA's declaration and bylaws, so that's the first document to check. Whoever ends up paying, the water still has to come out fast.

Upstairs neighbor leak: what to do

  • Knock or call your neighbor so they can shut off the water at the source.
  • Shut off your own water if the source isn't clear.
  • Call your HOA or property manager, especially if the neighbor isn't home; they may have access to shut-offs.
  • Turn off power to wet ceiling lights.
  • Photograph everything before cleanup.
  • Call for extraction and drying right away.
  • An upstairs neighbor leak usually comes through the ceiling first, then runs down walls. See ceiling water damage repair in Acworth

Shared wall leak and hidden damage

A shared wall leak is harder to spot because the plumbing is inside the wall between two units. Signs include:

  • Bubbling or peeling paint on the shared wall
  • Swollen baseboards along that wall
  • Damp carpet next to the wall with no visible source
  • A musty smell near the shared wall
  • Water stains on both sides of the wall
  • Thermal imaging finds where the water is in the wall cavity without opening both units unnecessarily.

An HO-6 policy is a condo or townhome owner's policy that covers your unit's interior, your belongings and liability. Master policies come in different types: some cover only the bare structure, others include interior finishes. Your declaration and both policies decide it.

HOA water leak in Acworth: coordinating the cleanup

An HOA water leak in Acworth often affects more than one owner. Clear coordination keeps it moving:

  • One moisture map showing every affected unit
  • Separate documentation for each owner's unit
  • Updates to the HOA or property manager, and to owners
  • Clear notes on where the leak started, which helps the insurance carriers sort out responsibility
  • Drying plans that work with each owner's access and schedule

Townhome Water Damage FAQs

What does an HO-6 policy cover?

+

An HO-6 policy usually covers your unit's interior, upgrades, personal belongings and liability, depending on what the HOA master policy covers.

Should I call my HOA first after a leak?

+

Stop the water first, then notify your HOA or property manager quickly. Many declarations require prompt notice.

My neighbor's leak damaged my unit. Who pays?

+

It depends on the cause and your HOA documents. Your neighbor's liability coverage, the master policy or your own HO-6 may apply.

Can you dry multiple units at once?

+

Yes. Each affected unit gets its own moisture readings and drying setup, coordinated with the owners and HOA.

How long does drying take in a townhome?

+

Usually 3 to 5 days, longer if shared walls, ceilings or several units are involved.
