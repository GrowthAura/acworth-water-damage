What causes a sagging, water-stained ceiling in two-story Acworth homes?

Usually an upstairs bathroom or laundry leak, an attic AC drain overflow or a roof leak after a storm soaking the drywall from above.

Many Acworth homes are two stories with bathrooms and laundry rooms upstairs and an air handler in the attic. That puts a lot of plumbing and condensate lines directly over living-room and kitchen ceilings. When any of them leaks, water spreads across the top of the drywall and soaks insulation before anything shows below. By the time a stain appears, the ceiling has often been wet for a while.

Is a sagging ceiling about to collapse?

It can be. A bulge in the ceiling usually means water is pooled on top of the drywall, and wet drywall loses strength quickly. If you see a sagging drywall ceiling:

  • Move people, pets and furniture out from under it.
  • Turn off power to any light fixtures or fans in that ceiling.
  • Place a bucket or tarp under the lowest point.
  • Don't press on it or poke it unless you're prepared for a sudden release of water.
  • Shut off the water to the bathroom or appliance above, or the main if you aren't sure.
  • Call for help so the water can be released and the cavity dried safely.

A sagging drywall ceiling can hold several gallons of water. Releasing it in a controlled way prevents a collapse that brings down drywall and wet insulation all at once.

Can a water stain on the ceiling just be painted over?

Only after the area is confirmed dry and the source is fixed. Painting over a water stain on a ceiling in Acworth without checking moisture can hide a leak that's still active, and wet drywall behind the paint can grow mold. Once the ceiling is dry and solid, the stain is sealed with a stain-blocking primer before painting so it doesn't bleed through. Drywall that's soft, crumbling or sagging needs to be replaced, not painted.

Upstairs bathroom leak damage

An upstairs bathroom leak is the most common cause of ceiling damage we see. It usually comes from:

  • A failed wax ring under the toilet
  • A cracked toilet supply line
  • Worn grout or caulk around a tub or shower
  • A leaking shower valve or drain inside the wall
  • An overflowing tub

Water from a toilet overflow is treated as contaminated, so ceiling materials below may need to be removed rather than dried. See sewage backup cleanup in Acworth

Attic leak damage

An attic leak can come from a roof problem, a clogged AC condensate line, a frozen pipe or a bathroom fan venting into the attic. Attic water soaks blown-in insulation, which holds moisture against the ceiling drywall. Wet insulation is usually removed and replaced, since it loses most of its insulating value once soaked and can grow mold.

Ceiling Water Damage FAQs

How long does a wet ceiling take to dry?

+

Usually 2 to 4 days with air movers and dehumidifiers, longer if insulation was soaked or framing got wet.

Why does the stain keep coming back after I paint?

+

The leak is still active or the drywall wasn't dry, or the stain wasn't sealed with a stain-blocking primer.

Will mold grow in my ceiling?

+

It can if wet drywall and insulation aren't dried within 24 to 48 hours. See mold remediation in Acworth

Is ceiling water damage covered by insurance?

+

Sudden leaks like a burst pipe or appliance failure are usually covered. Slow, long-term leaks and roof wear are often excluded.

Do you fix the leak itself?

+

The source is repaired by the right trade, like a plumber, roofer or HVAC tech. Our crew handles drying and ceiling repair.
