What should Acworth homeowners do when a storm drives water through the roof?

Get people away from sagging ceilings, catch dripping water, photograph the damage, then get the roof tarped and the attic and walls dried quickly.

Acworth gets strong thunderstorms in spring and summer, and many neighborhoods, especially along the Lake Allatoona corridor, have tall pines and hardwoods close to the house. That combination means roof damage often comes with more than one problem at once: an opening in the roof, soaked attic insulation, wet ceiling drywall and water running down inside exterior walls. Why emergency tarping comes first

Every hour a roof stays open, more rain gets in. Emergency tarping covers the damaged area with heavy-duty tarps secured past the opening, so drying inside can actually work. Most homeowners policies also expect you to take reasonable steps to prevent further damage after a storm, and emergency tarping is the most important one. Take photos of the roof before and after it's covered. The permanent roof repair is handled by a roofer once the weather clears.

Fallen tree roof damage

Fallen tree roof damage is one of the most serious storm calls in Acworth. A tree or large limb can crack rafters, punch through the decking and leave a wide opening for rain.

  • Stay out of rooms under the impact area; the structure may be weakened.
  • Don't climb on the roof or try to move the tree.
  • Call your insurer and document everything with photos.
  • Tree removal comes first, by a tree service, before the roof can be tarped.
  • Water cleanup starts as soon as the opening is covered.
  • With fallen tree roof damage, water often spreads across several rooms, so a full moisture map is essential.

Wind-driven rain and hidden leaks

Not all storm water comes through a hole. Wind-driven rain pushes water sideways under shingles, through flashing around chimneys and skylights, past window frames and through attic vents. These leaks can show up as:

  • A stain on a ceiling near an exterior wall
  • Water around window frames or under sills
  • Damp drywall low on exterior walls
  • Wet insulation near roof vents and ridge lines
  • Drips around skylights or chimney chases
  • Wind-driven rain leaks are easy to miss because they only show up in certain storms, which is how small leaks turn into mold.

Is storm damage covered by insurance?

Most Georgia homeowners policies cover sudden wind, hail and falling-tree damage to the roof, along with the resulting interior water damage. What's often not covered:

  • Leaks from an old or poorly maintained roof
  • Rising floodwater from outside, which needs flood coverage
  • Damage made worse because the roof wasn't covered after the storm
  • Photograph the roof, attic and every damaged room. Keep receipts for tarping and drying. See water damage insurance claim help in Acworth

Storm Damage FAQs

How fast can my roof be tarped?

+

A crew is dispatched immediately after your call. Tarping happens as soon as it's safe to get on the roof; tree removal comes first if a tree is still on it.

Does wet attic insulation need to be replaced?

+

Usually, yes. Soaked insulation loses most of its value, holds moisture against the ceiling and can grow mold.

Can I dry storm water damage myself?

+

Small, surface wetness maybe. Water in the attic, ceilings or walls needs moisture mapping and commercial drying to avoid mold.

Who fixes the roof?

+

A roofer handles the permanent roof repair. Our crew handles tarping, water removal, drying and interior repairs.

What if the storm knocked out power?

+

Drying equipment can run on a generator. In the meantime, extraction and removal of wet materials can still start.
