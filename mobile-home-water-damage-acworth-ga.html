Why does water damage spread faster in manufactured homes around Acworth?

Many manufactured homes use particleboard subfloors and a sealed underbelly, so leaks soak in fast and stay trapped unless the floor and belly are dried quickly.

Unlike a site-built home, a manufactured home's plumbing usually runs beneath the floor inside a fabric or plastic barrier called the bottom board or belly wrap. When a pipe leaks there, water collects in the belly instead of draining away, soaking insulation and the underside of the floor. Particleboard swells and loses strength when wet, which is why soft spots appear so quickly near tubs, toilets, kitchens and water heaters.

Mobile home subfloor water: soft floors and what they mean

Mobile home subfloor water damage usually shows up as soft, spongy or sagging floors. Common spots:

  • In front of the toilet and around the tub or shower
  • Under and in front of the kitchen sink
  • Around the water heater closet
  • Near the washer
  • Along exterior walls under windows
  • Along the marriage line in double-wide homes

Once particleboard swells, it rarely returns to full strength, so damaged sections are usually cut out and replaced with plywood or OSB after the framing is dried.

Belly board water: the hidden problem under the home

Belly board water, meaning water trapped in the bottom board, can sit for weeks. Signs include:

  • A sagging or bulging belly wrap under the home
  • Water dripping from the bottom board
  • Wet or fallen insulation hanging below
  • Musty odors inside, especially near floor vents
  • Higher water bills from a hidden supply line leak

The belly is opened at the wet area, water is drained, soaked insulation is removed, the area is dried and the bottom board is patched and resealed.

Skirting moisture and ventilation

Skirting closes off the space under the home. When it's damaged, missing vents or blocked, skirting moisture problems follow: ground moisture can't escape, humidity rises and condensation forms under the floor. Good ventilation and drainage under the home help keep the belly and subfloor dry. In winter, damaged skirting also lets cold air reach pipes, raising the risk of freezing.

Preventing frozen pipes in manufactured homes

  • Check that heat tape works before winter
  • Repair tears in the belly wrap
  • Keep skirting intact and close vents during hard freezes if recommended
  • Insulate the water supply line from the ground to the home
  • Let faucets drip on very cold nights
  • Keep heat on if the home will be empty

Mobile Home Water Damage FAQs

Can a soft mobile home floor be repaired?

+

Yes. The damaged particleboard is cut out and replaced with new decking after the framing underneath is dried.

What is the belly wrap?

+

It's the fabric or plastic barrier under a manufactured home that protects insulation and plumbing. Leaks can trap water inside it.

How do I know if water is in the belly?

+

Look for sagging or dripping belly wrap, wet insulation underneath, musty smells inside and a higher water bill.

Will my mobile home floor dry on its own?

+

Rarely. Trapped water in the belly and particleboard needs to be opened up and dried with equipment.
