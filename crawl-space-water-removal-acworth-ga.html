Why does standing water collect under homes in Acworth after storms?

Red-clay soil drains slowly, so storm runoff and plumbing leaks pool on crawl space floors, and vented crawls then pull in humid air that feeds mold.

Many Acworth homes built in the 1980s and 1990s sit on vented crawl spaces. When heavy rain saturates the clay soil, water seeps through foundation walls or rises up from the ground, and there's nowhere for it to drain. Gutters that dump next to the foundation and yards that slope toward the house make it worse. Standing water under house floors can sit unnoticed for weeks, while humidity climbs and wood stays damp.

Signs of a wet crawl space in Acworth, GA

  • A musty smell inside the house, especially on humid days
  • Floors that feel soft, spongy or uneven
  • Cupping hardwood or gaps in flooring above the crawl
  • Higher humidity, sticky air or condensation on windows
  • Insects like termites or camel crickets increasing
  • Sagging or wet insulation hanging from joists
  • Visible water or mud when you look through the access door
  • A wet crawl space in Acworth, GA affects the whole house, because air from the crawl space rises into the living areas above.

Vapor barrier water: what to do with a soaked liner

A vapor barrier is the plastic sheeting on the crawl space floor that keeps ground moisture from rising. When vapor barrier water pools on top of the plastic, it can't drain or evaporate, and it sits right under the floor framing. If water gets under the barrier, the plastic traps it against the soil.

  • Torn, muddy or contaminated barriers are removed and replaced.
  • Intact barriers may be lifted, drained, cleaned and reset once the soil is dry.
  • Seams and edges should be sealed so vapor barrier water doesn't reach the framing again.

Crawl space mold

Crawl spaces are dark, humid and full of wood, the perfect setup for mold. Crawl space mold usually shows up as white, gray or black growth on floor joists and the underside of the subfloor, often with a musty smell upstairs. Because mold in the crawl space can affect indoor air, it's handled with containment and removal, then the moisture problem is corrected so it doesn't return. See mold remediation in Acworth

Standing water under house floors: is it an emergency?

Yes, if there's more than a little dampness. Standing water under house floors raises humidity throughout the home, soaks insulation and keeps wood at moisture levels where rot and mold thrive. It can also attract termites and weaken floor framing over time. A burst pipe in a crawl space can put hundreds of gallons under the home quickly.

After drying: keeping the crawl space dry

Removing the water is the emergency step. Keeping it dry is the long-term fix. Common options include:

  • Extending downspouts and correcting yard grading
  • Installing or replacing the vapor barrier
  • Adding a sump pump for crawl spaces that take on groundwater
  • Crawl space encapsulation with a sealed liner and dehumidifier
  • Fixing plumbing and drain leaks under the floor
  • Permanent waterproofing and encapsulation are done by crawl space contractors once the space is dry.

Crawl Space Water FAQs

Can a wet crawl space cause mold upstairs?

+

Yes. Humid air from the crawl space moves up into the home, which can raise indoor humidity and contribute to musty odors and mold.

How long does it take to dry a crawl space?

+

Usually several days to a week, depending on how much water there was and how humid it is.

Do I need to replace crawl space insulation after water?

+

Usually yes. Wet insulation sags, loses its value and holds moisture against the subfloor.

Should I add a vapor barrier?

+

A properly installed vapor barrier helps keep ground moisture out. It should go in once the crawl space is dry.

Is water in my crawl space covered by insurance?

+

A burst pipe under the house is often covered. Groundwater seepage and outside flooding usually are not.
