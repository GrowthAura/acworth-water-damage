The first day after water gets into your home decides most of what happens next: how much can be saved, whether mold shows up and how smoothly your insurance claim goes. This checklist walks through exactly what to do, hour by hour, and why each step matters. Bookmark it now so it's ready when you need it.

What should you do first after water damage?

Stop the water, make the area safe, document everything and start drying within hours, because mold can begin growing on wet materials within 24 to 48 hours.

Everything else is secondary to those four steps. The EPA advises drying wet materials within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth, and in a humid place like Acworth, that window can feel even shorter. Speed is the biggest single factor in how big the job becomes.

Minutes 0 to 15: stop it and stay safe

  • Shut off the water. Close the valve behind the appliance or toilet, or the main shut-off where the water line enters the house.
  • Kill the power to wet areas. Only if you can reach the breaker without standing in water.
  • Get people and pets clear of sagging ceilings and standing water.
  • Don't touch sewage or floodwater. Outside water and sewage can carry bacteria.
  • If water is coming from the street or meter box, call Cobb County Water System Emergency Dispatch at 770-419-6201.

Minutes 15 to 60: document and protect

  • Photograph and video every wet area, including the source, before anything moves.
  • Move electronics, documents, rugs and valuables to a dry room.
  • Lift furniture off wet floors with foil or wood blocks under the legs.
  • Pull up area rugs so they don't stain the floor below.
  • Call for extraction and drying. In Acworth, a crew can be dispatched immediately.

Hours 1 to 6: get the water out

This is when professional extraction makes the biggest difference. Standing water is removed from carpet, hard floors and subfloors, and moisture is mapped with thermal imaging and meters so hidden water inside walls and under floors gets found. Materials that can't be dried, like soaked carpet pad or drywall that wicked water high, come out. See emergency water extraction in Acworth

Hours 6 to 24: start the drying

  • Dehumidifiers and air movers go in and run around the clock
  • Baseboards may be pulled so walls can dry from the bottom
  • Antimicrobial treatment goes on affected surfaces
  • First moisture readings are logged, which become your drying record
  • Notify your insurer and get a claim number

Clean water, gray water or sewage?

Clean water can turn into gray or black water if it sits a day or two, which is another reason the first 24 hours matter.

The insurance side of day one

Most Georgia homeowners policies cover sudden leaks like burst pipes and appliance failures, but they expect you to take reasonable steps to prevent more damage. Good day-one records help:

  • Photos and video before and after cleanup starts
  • The plumber's note on what failed
  • Moisture readings and drying logs
  • A list of damaged items
  • Receipts for emergency work
  • See water damage insurance claim help in Acworth

After the first day

Drying usually continues for about 3 to 5 days, with readings taken daily until every material reaches its dry goal. Repairs to drywall, flooring and finishes start only after that. If a leak raised your water bill, keep the repair receipt for the Cobb County leak credit.

FAQs

Should I turn off the electricity?

+

Turn off power to wet areas if you can reach the breaker from a dry spot. If not, stay out and wait for help.

Can I use fans to dry it myself?

+

Fans help only with very small, clean spills. For anything bigger, commercial dehumidifiers and moisture readings are needed to dry walls and floors.

How long until mold grows?

+

Mold can start within 24 to 48 hours on wet drywall, carpet and wood.
