Which parts of Acworth flood when Allatoona Creek and Lake Allatoona run high?

Low ground along Allatoona, Proctor, Clark and Pitner creeks and lake-edge homes near Lake Allatoona take on the most water after heavy, multi-day rain.

Acworth sits where several creeks feed Lake Allatoona, a U.S. Army Corps of Engineers reservoir built partly for flood control. When the lake runs high, creek water has nowhere to go quickly, and saturated ground around homes on sloped lots pushes water into basements, crawl spaces and garages. The National Weather Service tracks Allatoona Creek at its Stilesboro Road gauge, where flood stage is 13 feet. Flash flooding can also hit any neighborhood with poor drainage when heavy rain falls fast, even far from a creek.

What does flood damage restoration in Acworth, GA include?

  • Immediate dispatch with pumps, extractors and drying equipment.
  • Safety first: power to flooded areas is shut off and the structure is checked before anyone walks in.
  • Pump-out and extraction: submersible pumps move deep water; extractors pull the rest from floors.
  • Mud and debris removal: silt and debris are shoveled and cleaned out of floors, walls and HVAC returns.
  • Removal of unsalvageable materials: soaked drywall is cut above the water line, and wet insulation, carpet and pad are removed.
  • Cleaning and sanitizing: every surface that floodwater touched is cleaned and treated with antimicrobial.
  • Structural drying: dehumidifiers and air movers run with daily moisture readings until materials are dry.
  • Rebuild: drywall, insulation, flooring and trim are replaced once the structure passes dry readings.

Why is floodwater treated as Category 3 floodwater?

Water that enters from outside picks up soil, sewage overflow, fuel, lawn chemicals and animal waste on the way in. That's why outside flooding is always handled as Category 3 floodwater, the most contaminated class under the IICRC S500 standard.

What that means for your home:

  • Porous materials that touched the water, like carpet, pad, insulation and lower drywall, are usually removed, not dried.
  • Hard surfaces are cleaned and disinfected before drying starts.
  • Protective gear is needed during cleanup.
  • Children, pets and anyone with health concerns should stay out until the area is sanitized.

The category of the water decides what can stay and what has to go, so treating outside water as Category 3 floodwater from the start protects your family's health.

Storm flooding in Acworth homes

Not every flood comes from a creek. Storm flooding in Acworth often starts closer to the house:

  • Overflowing gutters dump roof water right against the foundation.
  • Poor grading sends yard runoff toward the house instead of away from it.
  • Window wells and basement stairwells fill and leak into lower levels.
  • Clogged yard drains and storm inlets back up during heavy rain.
  • Garage and driveway slopes channel water under doors.

What should you do right after a flooded house in Acworth?

  • Don't walk into standing water if power is still on in that area.
  • Shut off the main breaker only if you can reach it from a dry spot.
  • Avoid contact with floodwater; wear boots and gloves if you must go in.
  • Photograph and video every room before anything is moved.
  • Move dry valuables to upper floors.
  • Don't run the HVAC if returns or ductwork took on water.
  • Call for flood cleanup so pumping and sanitizing start right away.
  • A flooded house in Acworth gets harder to dry every hour the water sits, and mold can start within 24 to 48 hours.

Flood vs. water damage insurance in Georgia

The source of the water decides whether a claim is paid. Here's how flood vs water damage insurance usually breaks down:

SituationTypical coverage
Burst pipe, failed water heater, appliance leakHomeowners policy (sudden and accidental)
Creek or lake water entering the homeFlood policy, usually an NFIP flood policy
Heavy rain running in through the ground or doorsFlood policy
Sewer backupOften needs a sewer backup endorsement
Slow leak over weeks or monthsOften excluded as gradual damage

Standard Georgia homeowners policies exclude outside flooding. An NFIP flood policy through the National Flood Insurance Program covers flood damage to the structure and, with contents coverage, belongings. NFIP policies generally have a 30-day waiting period before coverage starts, so they need to be in place before a storm. Photos, moisture readings and drying records from the job help with any claim. How much does flood cleanup cost in Acworth?

Flood jobs usually cost more than clean-water leaks because more materials are removed and everything has to be sanitized. A smaller lower-level flood might stay in the low thousands, while full-basement or multi-room flooding with rebuild work can run well past $15,000.

Cost drivers include:

  • How deep the water got and how many rooms it reached
  • Amount of mud and debris to remove
  • Drywall, insulation, carpet and cabinets that must be replaced
  • Days of drying equipment needed
  • Whether mold has started

Flood Damage Restoration FAQs

Is floodwater dangerous to clean up myself?

+

Yes. Outside floodwater can carry sewage, bacteria and chemicals. Wear protective gear, avoid skin contact and keep children and pets away until the area is sanitized.

Does homeowners insurance cover flooding in Georgia?

+

Usually not. Outside flooding is excluded from standard policies and needs separate flood coverage, typically an NFIP flood policy.

How long does it take to dry a flooded home?

+

Most homes need about 3 to 5 days of drying after extraction and removal. Concrete, block walls and heavy flooding can take longer.

Do I need to remove drywall after a flood?

+

In most cases, drywall below the water line is cut out because it absorbs contaminated water. Framing behind it is usually cleaned and dried.

Can storm flooding in Acworth happen away from creeks?

+

Yes. Overflowing gutters, poor grading and clogged drains can flood a home anywhere in Acworth during heavy rain.
