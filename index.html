What causes water damage in Acworth homes?

Acworth sits between Lake Acworth and Lake Allatoona, with creeks, sloped lots and a large number of homes built in the 1980s and 1990s. That mix creates a few patterns that show up again and again:

  • Lake and creek water: Allatoona Creek, Proctor Creek, Clark Creek and Pitner Creek rise fast during multi-day rain, and saturated ground pushes water against lower levels.
  • Finished and walkout basements: Basements set into hillsides take in water where the wall meets the slab, through cinder-block seams or when a sump pump fails.
  • Attic and crawl space plumbing: Supply lines in unheated attics and vented crawl spaces are the first to freeze and split on cold nights.
  • Attic air handlers: Long Georgia cooling seasons fill condensate pans daily, and one clogged drain line can soak a ceiling.
  • Aging water heaters: Tanks in basements and garages often fail after about 8 to 12 years of use.
  • Sewer line blockages: Grease, roots and debris in a private sewer line can push wastewater back up through floor drains and toilets.
  • Storms and trees: Wind-driven rain and falling limbs open roofs and let water into attics and upper floors.

What are the hidden signs of water damage in an Acworth home?

Not every leak announces itself with a puddle. Watch for:

  • A musty smell in a finished basement that doesn't fade with the AC running
  • Paint bubbling or peeling on walls near bathrooms, laundry rooms or exterior walls
  • Baseboards swelling, separating or turning soft at the bottom
  • Hardwood boards cupping, or laminate and LVP lifting at the seams
  • White, chalky residue (efflorescence) on cinder-block basement walls
  • Brown rings or soft spots on ceilings below bathrooms or under the attic air handler
  • A water meter that keeps moving with every fixture turned off
  • Higher indoor humidity, condensation on windows or doors that suddenly stick

Any of these is worth a moisture check before it turns into a larger repair.

Who pays for water damage in Acworth: you, Cobb County or insurance?

Water and sewer lines

Cobb County Water System maintains the public water and sewer mains. Pipes on your property, including your private sewer line, are the homeowner's responsibility. If you see a break in the street or suspect a county sewer main is backing up, Cobb Water's Emergency Dispatch line is 770-419-6201.

Cobb County leak credit

Cobb County Water System offers a partial leak credit once in a rolling 12-month period for documented private plumbing leaks, covering no more than two billing months. Keep your plumbing repair receipt, because it is required to apply.

Homeowners insurance vs. flood insurance

Most Georgia homeowners policies cover sudden and accidental water damage, such as a burst pipe or a failed water heater, but they exclude outside floodwater and long-term seepage. Flooding from creeks or rising lake water usually needs a separate flood policy through the NFIP. Photos, moisture readings and drying logs help support any claim. What should you do in the first hour after water damage?

  • Shut off the water at the main valve or the appliance supply.
  • Turn off power to wet rooms if it's safe to reach the breaker.
  • Take photos and video of every wet area before moving anything.
  • Move valuables, electronics and rugs to a dry room.
  • Stay out of sewage or floodwater, which can carry bacteria.
  • Don't use a household vacuum on standing water.
  • Call for water extraction so drying can start right away.
