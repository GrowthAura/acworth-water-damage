Why do upstairs laundry rooms cause so much water damage in Acworth homes?

A burst washer hose can release gallons every minute, and upstairs laundry rooms let water travel through floors into ceilings and walls below.

Many newer homes around Acworth, especially in 30102 communities like Cherokee Township and Worthington, put the laundry room on the second floor next to the bedrooms. It's convenient, but when an upstairs laundry leak happens, water runs through the subfloor into the ceiling of the kitchen or living room below. Without a drain pan and a shut-off, a supply hose burst can release hundreds of gallons before anyone gets home.

Washing machine leak in Acworth: what to do

  • A washing machine leak in Acworth is one of the most damaging indoor leaks because the supply lines are under constant pressure.
  • Turn off the water valves behind the washer, or the main if you can't reach them.
  • Unplug the washer if you can do it from a dry spot.
  • Check the ceiling below if the laundry is upstairs.
  • Move items off the floor nearby.
  • Call for water removal so drying starts quickly.
  • Washing machine water from the drain is Category 2 gray water, so floors and lower walls are cleaned before drying.

Dishwasher leak water damage in Acworth

Dishwasher leak water damage in Acworth is often slow and hidden. Water seeps under the dishwasher and into the cabinets and subfloor, where it can sit for weeks. Signs include:

  • Warped or swollen flooring next to the dishwasher
  • Soft or delaminating cabinet bottoms
  • A musty smell from under the sink
  • Water stains on the ceiling below the kitchen
  • Cleanup usually means pulling the dishwasher out, removing toe kicks and drying under cabinets and flooring.

Refrigerator leak in Acworth homes

A refrigerator leak in Acworth usually comes from the ice maker line, a cracked filter housing or a clogged defrost drain. Ice maker line leaks are slow but steady and can soak hardwood or LVP in front of the fridge for weeks. Plastic ice maker lines crack over time; braided stainless lines hold up better. Check for water under the fridge any time the floor nearby feels soft or the boards start to cup. See hardwood floor water damage in Acworth

How to prevent appliance leaks

  • Replace rubber washer hoses with braided stainless hoses
  • Replace washer hoses about every 5 years, or sooner if bulging or cracked
  • Put upstairs washers in a drain pan with a drain line
  • Install water leak sensors near the washer, dishwasher, fridge and water heater
  • Consider an automatic shut-off valve for laundry rooms
  • Leave space behind the washer so hoses don't kink
  • Turn off washer supply valves when away for long trips

Appliance Leak FAQs

How often should washer hoses be replaced?

+

About every 5 years for rubber hoses, or right away if they bulge, crack or show rust at the fittings.

Can water under a dishwasher cause mold?

+

Yes. Water trapped under cabinets and flooring can grow mold within 24 to 48 hours if it isn't dried.

Is washing machine water dirty?

+

Supply water is clean, but drain water is gray water and needs cleaning before drying.

Do I need to remove my cabinets?

+

Usually not. Toe kicks are removed and cabinets are dried in place unless the boxes are swollen or delaminated.

Will a leak sensor really help?

+

Yes. Sensors alert you to small leaks early, and some connect to automatic shut-off valves.
