What should you do first when a pipe bursts in an Acworth home?

Shut off the main water valve, turn off power to wet rooms, move valuables and get extraction started before water spreads under floors and into walls.

The main shut-off valve is usually where the water line enters the house: in the basement, a utility room, the garage, a crawl space access or near the water heater. Some Acworth homes also have a curb shut-off at the meter box near the street. Find yours before you need it, because the first five minutes after a pipe bursts decide how much of the house gets wet.

Where do pipes burst in Acworth homes?

Many Acworth homes were built in the 1980s and 1990s, in neighborhoods like Bells Ferry Place, Bennett Farms and Abbotts Glen, and their plumbing runs through spaces that get cold:

  • Frozen pipes in attic spaces: supply lines to upstairs bathrooms often pass through unheated attics.
  • Crawl spaces: vented crawl spaces drop close to outdoor temperatures on hard freezes.
  • Exterior walls: pipes to kitchen sinks and hose bibs on outside walls freeze first.
  • Garages: lines feeding garage water heaters and laundry rooms.
  • Under sinks and behind appliances: supply line failure on braided hoses, toilet lines and ice maker lines.
  • Frozen pipes in attic areas are especially costly, because the water comes down through ceiling drywall, insulation and light fixtures into the rooms below.

Frozen pipe burst vs. supply line failure

CauseHow it happensWhen it shows up
Frozen pipe burstIce expands and splits the pipe; water flows once it thawsOften hours after the cold snap, when temperatures rise
Supply line failureBraided or rubber hose to a toilet, sink, washer or ice maker rupturesAny time, often with no warning
Fitting or joint failureCrimp ring, solder joint or compression fitting lets goAny time, often in older plumbing
Pinhole leakSmall leak that runs slowly inside a wallWeeks later: stains, odor, high water bill

A frozen pipe burst in Acworth usually reveals itself as the day warms up, which is why many calls come mid-morning after an overnight freeze. Supply line failure can happen any day of the year.

Signs of a hidden pipe leak in your Acworth home

Not every pipe leak water damage case in Acworth starts with a burst. Slow leaks hide inside walls and under floors:

  • A water bill that jumps with no change in use
  • The water meter moving with every fixture off
  • Damp drywall, bubbling paint or swollen baseboards
  • Brown stains or sagging spots on ceilings
  • A musty smell that won't go away
  • Warm spots on a slab floor (hot water line leak)
  • Cupping hardwood or LVP lifting at the seams

Who pays for a burst pipe: you or Cobb County?

Cobb County Water System is responsible for the public water mains. Pipes on your side of the meter, inside and outside the house, belong to the homeowner. If the break is in the street or at the main, call Cobb Water's Emergency Dispatch at 770-419-6201.

If a burst pipe on your property drove your water bill up, Cobb County Water System has a leak credit policy:

  • A partial credit once in a rolling 12-month period
  • Covers no more than two affected billing months
  • Requires a plumbing repair receipt or proof of the repair
  • You still pay the bill while the credit is processed
  • Save every receipt from the plumber and the restoration work. See Cobb County water leak credit guide

Does homeowners insurance cover burst pipe water damage in Acworth?

Most Georgia homeowners policies cover sudden and accidental burst pipes, including the water damage to walls, floors and ceilings. What often isn't covered:

  • Repairing the broken pipe itself
  • Slow leaks that went on for weeks or months
  • Damage in a home left without heat, when the policy requires heat or a drained system during vacancy
  • Document everything with photos and keep moisture readings and drying logs. See water damage insurance claim help in Acworth

Burst pipes in vacant lake homes and second homes

Second homes near Lake Allatoona and homes left empty over the holidays are the riskiest. A pipe can freeze, split and run for days before anyone notices, soaking multiple floors and starting mold. If a home will sit empty in winter:

  • Keep the heat on at a steady setting
  • Shut off the main water and drain the lines, or have the system winterized
  • Open cabinet doors on exterior walls
  • Have someone check the house after hard freezes

When bringing a winterized home back into use, follow the right steps. Cobb County Water System warns that restoring water to a home that wasn't properly de-winterized can cause leaking pipes. See Lake Allatoona water damage restoration

How to prevent the next burst pipe

  • Insulate pipes in attics, crawl spaces and garages
  • Seal gaps where cold air reaches plumbing in exterior walls
  • Let faucets on exterior walls drip on nights in the low 20s or colder
  • Disconnect garden hoses and cover hose bibs before the first freeze
  • Replace rubber supply hoses with braided lines, and replace braided lines that show rust or bulges
  • Learn where your main shut-off valve is and make sure it turns
  • See frozen pipe prevention for Acworth homes

Burst Pipe Water Damage FAQs

How long does a burst pipe take to dry out?

+

Most burst pipe jobs take about 3 to 5 days of drying after extraction. Ceilings with soaked insulation and multi-floor leaks can take longer.

Should I turn off the water heater after a pipe bursts?

+

If you shut off the main water supply for a long period, turn the water heater off or to vacation mode to protect it.

Can I use the house while it dries?

+

In most cases, yes. Drying equipment is loud but safe. Rooms with removed ceilings or open walls may need to stay closed off.

Will mold grow after a burst pipe?

+

It can within 24 to 48 hours if wet materials aren't dried. Fast extraction and measured drying are the best prevention. See mold remediation in Acworth
