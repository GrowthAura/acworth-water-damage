Who handles water damage in an Acworth rental home: the landlord or the tenant?

Landlords usually handle the structure and repairs, while a tenant's renters insurance covers their belongings. Tenants should report leaks right away, in writing.

In Georgia, landlords are generally required to keep rental property in repair, so the building, plumbing and fixtures are typically the owner's responsibility. Tenants are generally responsible for damage they cause through negligence and for their own belongings. The lease spells out details like how to report problems and who handles what, so both sides should check it. When water is coming in, the first priority for everyone is the same: stop it and dry it fast.

What tenants should do when they find a leak

  • Shut off the water at the fixture or main if you can.
  • Turn off power to wet areas if it's safe.
  • Notify your landlord or property manager right away, and follow up in writing by text or email.
  • Photograph the damage and your belongings.
  • Move your belongings out of the water.
  • Contact your renters insurance about your property.
  • Prompt written notice protects tenants and gives the landlord the chance to act before damage spreads.

What landlords and property managers should do

  • Get extraction and drying started right away.
  • Coordinate access with the tenant.
  • Call a plumber or roofer to fix the source.
  • Notify your landlord policy insurer and keep the claim number.
  • Decide on temporary arrangements if the home isn't livable.
  • Keep records of photos, moisture logs and invoices.
  • Landlord water damage in Acworth handled within the first day usually means smaller repairs and a shorter disruption for the tenant.

Renters insurance and the tenant's belongings

Renters insurance covers a tenant's belongings, not the building. It may also help with temporary living costs if the home becomes unlivable, depending on the policy. Landlord policies cover the structure but usually not the tenant's property. Tenants without renters insurance are typically responsible for replacing their own belongings.

Vacant rental leaks between tenants

Vacant rental leaks are some of the costliest losses, because nobody is there to notice. Between tenants:

  • Keep heat on in winter, or have the plumbing winterized
  • Check the property after hard freezes and big storms
  • Install leak sensors near water heaters, washers and under sinks
  • Shut off the water main if the unit will sit empty for a long time
  • Check under sinks, around toilets and the water heater during turnover
  • Long vacancies may also affect coverage under some landlord policies. See burst pipe water damage in Acworth

Mold in rentals

Mold is often the issue that creates conflict between tenants and landlords. Fast reporting, fast drying and fixing the moisture source prevent most of it. When mold is found, containment and removal come first, along with correcting the leak or humidity that caused it. See mold remediation in Acworth

Rental Property Water Damage FAQs

Does renters insurance cover water damage?

+

Renters insurance usually covers a tenant's belongings damaged by a sudden leak, not the building itself.

What should a tenant do first after a leak?

+

Stop the water if possible, notify the landlord right away in writing and photograph the damage and belongings.

Can repairs be done with the tenant still living there?

+

Often, yes. Drying equipment can run while tenants stay, unless sewage, mold or major repairs make the home unsafe.

Can you work with property managers?

+

Yes. Updates, photos and moisture logs are shared with the owner or property manager throughout the job.

Who pays if the tenant caused the leak?

+

It depends on the lease, the cause and the policies involved. The landlord's policy may cover the building and seek recovery from the tenant.
