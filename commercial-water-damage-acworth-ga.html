How do Acworth businesses keep operating after a water loss?

Crews extract water and set drying equipment after hours or in sections, so offices, shops and restaurants can reopen as soon as it's safe.

A water loss at a business is different from one at home. There are customers, employees, inventory, records and often a landlord or property manager involved. The plan is built around keeping as much of the space open as possible: isolating the wet area, running equipment overnight or on weekends and staging repairs so unaffected parts of the building keep working. After-hours drying and phased work

After-hours drying lets equipment run overnight and on weekends when the space is empty, with the most disruptive work scheduled outside business hours. Other ways to keep a business open:

  • Containment walls to separate the work area from customers
  • Drying one section at a time in larger spaces
  • Moving equipment out of customer paths during open hours
  • Quieter equipment setups in offices and medical suites
  • Clear daily updates for owners, managers and landlords

Tenant build-out and landlord coordination

In multi-tenant buildings, responsibility for the repair often depends on the lease. Generally, landlords cover the building shell and shared systems, while tenants may cover their own build-out. When a tenant build-out is damaged, documenting what belongs to whom saves time with both insurance carriers. Water often travels between suites through shared walls and ceilings, so neighboring units are checked too.

Restaurant and retail leaks

Restaurant and retail leaks have their own problems. In restaurants, floor drains, grease traps, dishwashers and ice machines are frequent sources, and food areas need cleaning and sanitizing before reopening. In retail stores, roof leaks and sprinkler breaks can soak inventory and fixtures. Fast extraction and moving stock off the floor limit losses. Commercial Water Damage FAQs

Can drying happen while we stay open?

+

Often, yes. With containment, phased work and after-hours drying, many businesses keep part or all of the space open.

Who pays for water damage in a leased space?

+

It depends on the lease and the cause. Landlords usually cover the building, while tenants may cover their own build-out and contents.

How long does commercial drying take?

+

Most commercial spaces dry in about 3 to 5 days. Large spaces, concrete and contaminated water can take longer.
